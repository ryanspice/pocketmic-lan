Choose the input on purpose.

Use Voice for the phone’s voice-communication path, or try Clean when you want a less-processed source. Device support varies.

Make a short test recording.

Speak, pause, and clap once on camera. Review the recording for level, background noise, and audio/video alignment.

Capture the source only once.

Avoid adding the same microphone both globally and as a scene source. Duplicate capture can produce an echo.

The route, step by step.

  1. Get audio into Windows.
    Install and pair PocketMic, then confirm audio is reaching the Windows receiver.
  2. Route through VB-CABLE.
    Install VB-CABLE separately. Choose CABLE Input as the receiver’s playback destination.
  3. Add an OBS audio input.
    Add an Audio Input Capture source to the scene and choose CABLE Output. Keep the same device disabled as a duplicate global input.
  4. Record before going live.
    Record a short sample and review it. Check the level and audio/video alignment; do not assume the network audio arrives at the same time as your camera.
Know the boundary.

48 kHz describes the audio sample rate, not a guarantee of studio quality. Phone hardware, placement, room acoustics, buffering, and your OBS settings all matter. Not intended for live instrument monitoring.

Before you commit.

Does PocketMic stream directly to an audience?

No. PocketMic transports the microphone audio to your Windows PC. OBS or your streaming app controls recording, broadcasting, and any internet upload.

What do I download?

The Android debug-signed APK and the Windows receiver ZIP from the same PocketMic release. VB-CABLE is a separate download. See the exact files.

What should I do if there is no sound?

Check the path in order: phone capture, receiver packets, receiver output, then your app’s microphone. Use the troubleshooting guide.
