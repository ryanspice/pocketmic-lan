03 — WHY

Built like a tool, not a subscription.

01

A native Android transmitter

PocketMic captures audio in its Android app and sends it to the Windows receiver over your local network. The phone needs the app and microphone permission.

02

PocketMic sends directly over your LAN

The phone and computer use encrypted UDP on your local network; internet access is not required for PocketMic audio. A calling or recording app you route into may process or transmit that audio under its own service and privacy terms.

03

Choose a Windows audio route

The receiver plays to a selected Windows device. To feed a call or recording app, select a virtual audio cable such as VB-CABLE, then choose its input in that app.

04

Designed for a private LAN

The phone and PC communicate directly with encrypted audio packets. Internet access is not needed for audio transport; use a trusted private network and do not expose the receiver to the public internet.

04 — ARCHITECTURE

The whole path, on your network.

The Android transmitter sends encrypted UDP audio over your LAN to the Windows receiver. The receiver plays the stream to a selected Windows audio device. Internet access is not required for the audio path.

PROTOCOL

Audio facts, without guessed latency.

No dated, reproducible end-to-end latency result is established for the current release candidate. These values describe protocol framing and receiver configuration; they are not a physical-device performance benchmark.

48 kHz

Mono sample rate

Both current packet formats use 48 kHz mono audio.

10 ms

Audio frame

Each packet carries 480 mono samples.

768 kbit/s

PCM audio payload

Raw PCM16 audio only; the encrypted PocketMic datagram is 800 kbit/s before UDP/IP overhead.

32–48 kbit/s

Opus target bitrate

Protocol v2 target. The macOS preview currently supports PCM v1 only.

Receiver buffering

Playback starts after a 100 ms prebuffer by default. The prebuffer can be adjusted from 40 to 300 ms; the 220 ms high-water mark applies at the default setting and is derived from the selected prebuffer.

Packet-loss concealment

The receiver can conceal gaps of up to 20 packets, or 200 ms, by repeating a decaying last-good frame. This is a recovery limit, not a latency figure or guarantee.

PCM v1 packet size

One encrypted datagram is 1,000 bytes: 24-byte header, 960-byte PCM16 ciphertext, and 16-byte authentication tag. UDP/IP overhead is additional.

PCM v1 frame and packet details
24 B960 B · AUDIO16 B

A PCM16 frame contains 480 mono samples at 48 kHz: 10 ms of audio, sent 100 times per second. Opus v2 carries the same 10 ms frame duration with a variable-length encrypted payload.

SECURITY

The PocketMic link stays on your network.

PocketMic encrypts and authenticates audio datagrams with AES-256-GCM. No account or cloud relay is required for the phone-to-PC connection.

Use a trusted private LAN. Do not port-forward the receiver; it is not a hardened internet voice service. The app you route audio into can have its own network and privacy behaviour.
