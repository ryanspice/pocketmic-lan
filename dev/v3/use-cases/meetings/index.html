Before the call.

Try your Android phone as an alternative microphone for Teams, Zoom, or another Windows calling app. Set it up and test it before you need it — not in the first minute of an important meeting.

A practical experiment for your desk setup.

PocketMic is a good fit for people who are comfortable installing an Android APK and a Windows audio driver. It is early software, so keep a known-working microphone available while you evaluate it.

Check your work-device rules.

APK installs and audio-driver changes may be restricted on managed devices. Use approved software and ask your administrator before installing.

Run the destination app’s test.

A moving phone meter is not proof that the meeting app can hear you. Check the selected input and run a test call or recording.

The route, step by step.

  1. Prepare the devices.
    Install PocketMic on Android and Windows on devices where you are allowed to do so. Connect both to the same trusted private network.
  2. Connect the audio route.
    With VB-CABLE installed, send PocketMic Receiver to CABLE Input.
  3. Set the calling app’s input.
    Choose CABLE Output as the microphone in your calling app. In Teams, look in Settings → Devices and its audio settings.
  4. Make a test call first.
    Use Teams’ test-call control where available, or your destination app’s input test. Confirm speech, silence, and your preferred listening output before the real call.
Know the boundary.

This is not positioned as a production-supported conferencing system or an emergency fallback you can trust without testing. Your meeting provider may transmit, process, or record the audio after it leaves PocketMic.

Before you commit.

Will this keep a Teams or Zoom call off the internet?

No. Only the PocketMic phone-to-PC transport stays on your private network. The meeting app still handles the call under its own network, privacy, and recording behaviour.

What do I download?

The Android debug-signed APK and the Windows receiver ZIP from the same PocketMic release. VB-CABLE is a separate download. See the exact files.

What should I do if there is no sound?

Check the path in order: phone capture, receiver packets, receiver output, then your app’s microphone. Use the troubleshooting guide.
